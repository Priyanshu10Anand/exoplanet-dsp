Digital Signal Processing: The conversion of raw analog signals from celestial objects into numerical data streams that computers can clean, filter, and analyze.

Telemetry: automated collection of measurements and data from remote spacecraft, satellites, or telescopes, and its wireless transmission back to Earth for monitoring and analysis.

Photometry: technique of measuring the flux or intensity of light radiated by celestial objects.

White Noise: Random signal that has equal intensity across all frequencies, resulting in a flat and constant power spectral density.

1/f colored noise: Also called Pink Noise is random, time-correlated signal fluctuation where the power spectral density S(f) is inversely proportional to the frequency (f) of the signal. It is "colored" because it has more power at lower frequencies.

Pre-whitening: Iterative data-processing technique used to extract individual periodic oscillation frequencies (such as stellar pulsation modes) from noisy time-series data.

Matched Filtering: Signal processing technique used to detect faint, known signal patterns hidden inside loud observational noise by correlating the noisy data with a theoretical template of the signal.

Coherent Stacking: Data processing technique where multiple individual signals or observations are aligned by both their amplitude and phase before being added together constructively

lightkurve: Open-source Python package designed to analyze astronomical flux time-series data. It provides a user-friendly API to download, inspect and analyze data collected by NASA’s Kepler, K2 and TESS exoplanet missions

astropy: open-source Python library used for astronomical data analysis, calculations and research

numpy: open-source library for foundational mathematical operations and data analysis in Python

scipy: open-source Python library used for advanced scientific, mathematical and technical computing and algorithms.

matplotlib: open-source Python library used for creating static, animated, and interactive data visualizations

Cadence: Time interval of how often an instrument observes a specific target.

Simple Aperture Photometry (SAP): Measurement of a target star's brightness calculated by summing the raw light values of all the pixels within the aperture on a digital sensor.

Pre-search Data Conditioning Simple Aperture Photometry (PDCSAP): Corrected version of the SAP data that has been algorithmically cleaned to remove instrumental noise, spacecraft artifacts and long-term systematic errors while preserving short-term variations like exoplanet transits.

Pointing Jitter: Rapid, uncommanded micro-vibration of a telescope's line-of-sight over short time intervals (usually s or ms)

Thermal focus drift: Gradual loss of sharp focus in telescope lens caused by changing temperatures during an observation session

Cotrending Basis Vectors: Set of mathematical reference profiles used in astronomy to remove instrumental noise and systematic errors from space telescope light curves

Reaction-wheel desaturation event: Also called "momentum dumping" or "momentum unloading" is a scheduled operational procedure where a spacecraft slows down its rapidly spinning reaction wheels to shed accumulated angular momentum.
